# Zoned Combat for Dungeons & Dragons

## Introduction

Basic rules for using combat without a grid, using mostly theatre of the mind but with abstract zones.

Combat occurs simultaneously, with all participants constantly moving; abstracting using zones is as valid as abstracting use step by step individual turns.

## Zones

Combat is divided up into a close range zone (near) and a long range zone (far) for each side. There is also an old-of-combat zone for each side.

The close range zone is not static, but moves focus as characters reposition; moving towards an enemy will move them into the close range zone, i.e. shift the focus.

All melee combat takes place in the close range zone. Generally there is just one melee, with all characters in range of each other, although in some cases you may decide there are two separate melee groups (but feel free to merge them after a round or two as the chaos of battle takes hold).

Generally creatures start in the close range zone, although sometimes in a open area you may allow them to start at range. A creature's first move may be to long range, representing them positioning away from the combat.

## Movement

General it takes a normal move action to move between zones, and to reposition within a zone such as engaging in melee combat (or leaving melee combat).

**Move actions required**

| Speed | Reposition | Switch Zones | Switch Zones and Reposition | Move in/out of combat |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| 15 ft or less | 2 | 2 | 4 | 4 |
| 20 ft - 45 ft | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 50 ft or more | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

Normally moving from long range to close range and then repositioning to engage in melee takes two moves (a Move plus the Dash actions), so you can't Attack. Special abilities, e.g. Dash as a bonus action, may be used to also allow you to Attack. If a creatures speed is 50 ft or more, then they can both switch zones and reposition as part of their move, and still Attack.

Repositioning out of melee combat (to the close range zone) risks attack of opportunity as normal. You can Disengage, to avoid, and the Move to reposition as normal; or Move and Dash to get to long range. You will need a special ability to do all three, e.g. Disengage as a bonus action, Move to reposition, and Dash to get to long range.

Moving from out of combat to the long range zone (or vice versa) usually takes two normal moves.

### Moving focus

If you move from the close range towards an enemy in long range, then the focus of the combat shifts -- you don't move onto their side, but they are moved into the close range zone. You could then use a second move, i.e. your entire turn, to engage them in melee combat.

This does also bring them closer to your allies, which represents in the chaos of simultaneous battle either you herding or pinning down that enemy, or allies just moving to keep up. This keeps the combat simple with only close and long zones to worry about.

## Range

The following ranges are used when attacking enemies, to determine if the target is in range, or if any penalities (e.g. disadvantage) apply.

| Situation | Range |
| -- | -- |
| Melee combat | touch / 0 ft |
| Same zone | 20 ft |
| Long range to close range | 50 ft |
| Long range to long range | 100 ft |

They are also used when casting spells at range on allies, e.g. casting from long range to close range, or cast from close range to an ally engaged in melee (when the caster is not engaged).

You can also cast a touch spell on an ally in the same zone and not engaged in melee by taking a Move to reposition.

Cast spells with a range of only 15 ft (or less, e.g. touch) on allies in melee combat requires that you also enter melee combat.
