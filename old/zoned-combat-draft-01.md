## Zones

Basic setup consists of a close combat zone, a ranged zone on each side (ally side, enemy side), and an outside zone for things outside of the combat.

Within the close combat zone, creatures are either disengaged or engaged in the melee. There is only one melee, which is a whirling chaos of participants, and the focus of heroic combat.

## Start of combat

You normally decide at the start of combat if you start in the close combat zone or your ranged zone.

In some cases starting zones may be limited, e.g. for an enclosed area the game master may decide all participants may start in the close combat zone, or if fighting from opposite sides of a ravine all participants may start in their ranged zones.

The game master may also set other limitations, for example a narrow or constrained area such as a bridge or doorway may be limited to a maximum of two creatures per side in the close combat zone.

## Ranges

The distance from a ranged zone to close combat, or vice versa, counts as 50 feet range. From the ally ranged zone to the enemy ranged zone counts as 100 feet.

This means if you have a normal 30 feet speed moving between adjacent zones requires the Dash action.

## Engagement

If you are engaged in the melee, you may make melee attacks against any other creature also engaged in the melee.

If you are in the close combat zone you may use movement equal to half your speed to engage a creature not already engaged, pulling them into the melee. You can't engage if you don't have enough movement left or if your speed is 0.

If you were also not engaged then this movement makes you engaged.

If you were already engaged, then this movement provokes opportunity attacks.

The disengage action, Mobile feat, or other similar effects can be used to avoid provoking opportunity attacks as normal. Likewise, feats such as Sentinel that prevent movement will also prevent engaging.

If at any time the melee consists of creatures from only one side of the battle, they all become disengaged.

## Tactics check

Many events may allow you a chance to get out of the melee with movement, e.g. if an enemy intentionally moves away, is killed, or is pushed, pulled, or otherwise forcibly moved, then that may provide the needed opportunity.

For example, the fighter may kill the goblin next to the wizard, so that the wizard is no longer threatened (no longer in the melee).

When such an opportunity arises, you may use your reaction to make an opposed Intelligence check (a tactics check) against another enemy in the melee chosen by the game master.

If you equal or exceed the enemy check, you are no longer in the melee.
