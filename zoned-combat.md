# Zoned Combat for Dungeons & Dragons

## Introduction

Basic rules for using combat without a grid, e.g. theatre of the mind, using abstract zones.

Combat occurs simultaneously, with all participants constantly moving; abstracting using zones is as valid as abstracting use step by step individual turns.

## Zones

1. Close Combat zone
  - Engaged (the Melee)
  - Disengaged
2. Ranged zone
  - Each side has their own Ranged zone
3. Out of Range zone

## Start of combat

Decide which zone each participant is in at the start of combat, either the Close Combat zone, their Ranged zone, or Out of Range, based on the circumstances, terrain, marching order, etc.

## Moving between zones

- Moving from Ranged to Close Combat, or vice-versa, costs 30 ft of movement.
- Moving from Ranged to Out of Range, or vice-versa, costs 60 ft of movement.

**Comments:** Changing from Ranged to Close Combat takes all of the movement for a normal character, leaving no movement available to Engage (see below). Characters with access to additional movement speed can both change zones and engage. Characters may also Dash to engage, giving up their normal action.

## Engaging

A creature in the Close Combat zone and not currently engaged may engage another creature by spending movement. If successfully engaged, both characters enter the Melee.

Engagement movement costs:

- Someone already in the Melee: 10 ft of movement
- Someone in the Close Combat zone: 20 ft of movement
- Someone in their Ranged zone: 50 ft of movement, and the opponent may try to avoid

Engaging someone, even in the Ranged zone, brings them into the Melee, in the Close Combat zone (forcing them to change zone).

**Comments:** To keep things simple there is a single Close Combat zone, and a single Melee. This means if your ally engages one enemy and you engage another then you both end up in the same melee and can attack either creature. This abstraction represents the chaotic nature of combat with all creatures simultaneously moving and fighting; it becomes one abstract fight.

Similarly when you Engage a creature in their Ranged zone, they get pulled into the Close Combat zone and into the Melee. This also represents the abstract nature of combat; they lose their ranged advantage.

It is not necessarily that the new creature has been moved into the melee, but may be that the melee has expanded/moved to where the creature is.

Note that engaging someone who is in their Ranged zone requires a lot of movement, so you generally need either access to additional movement, or to give up your action by Dashing.

### Avoiding engagement: tactics check

Someone in the Ranged zone can try to avoid being Engaged by tactical maneuvering.

They reduce their speed by 20 ft, if they have enough available, to reflect the maneuvering effort.

They can then make an Intelligence saving throw to avoid becoming Engaged. The DC equals 8 plus the engaging creatures Intelligence modifier and Proficiency Bonus.

**Comments:** Rather than automatically losing their ranged advantage, a creature has a chance to avoid engagement. The saving throw is based on Intelligence to reflect the tactical nature of maneuvering.

Because avoiding costs speed creatures can generally only do it once per turn, unless they have access to a higher speed. i.e. if they are swarmed they will get pulled into melee.

### Moving out of melee

Leaving the Melee costs 10 ft of movement, and makes you Disengaged, but still in the Close Combat zone.

Creatures in the Melee may make Opportunity Attacks as normal, as you leave their reach.

Once you are Disengaged you can use any remaining movement to Engage another, bringing them into the Melee as well.

You may use actions as normal, e.g. the Disengage action to avoid Opportunity Attacks or the Dash action to get additional movement.

**Comments:** A creature may move out of melee, spending 10 ft of movement, then immediately engage another creature in the close combat zone, spending 20 ft over movement, and end up back in melee (having dragged another creature in).

However they risk an Opportunity Attack unless they have access to special movement or give up their action to Disengage.

You can also move out of melee and then move from the Close Combat zone to the Ranged zone; or move out of melee and then Engage a creature from their Ranged zone; however both options require access to additional movement (and risk Opportunity Attacks).

### Opportunity attacks

Opportunity attacks are made as normal when a creature leaves the Melee.

For a melee with multiple opponents on both sides it is reasonable to limit Opportunity Attacks to one, plus one for each opposing creature that outnumbers the other side. e.g. if two heroes are fighting two monsters, then only one would get an Opportunity Attack; if there were three monsters, then two would get an attack.

### Other ways to leave melee

#### Taking an opponent out

Once per turn when and an opponent is defeated, then one ally may make an Intelligence saving throw (representing tactics) to leave Close Combat. The DC is 8 + Intelligence modifier + Proficiency Bonus set by one opponent.

If you are outnumbered or there are other factors, the GM may impose disadvantage on this save.

**Comments:** This represents killing the creature engaging an ally, leaving them free. An example of when disadvantage might apply is if the enemy have reach.

#### Forced movement

Once per turn when an opponent is pushed 5 ft or more, then one ally may make an Intelligence saving throw (representing tactics) to leave Close Combat. The DC is 8 + Intelligence modifier + Proficiency Bonus set by one opponent.

If you are outnumbered or there are other factors, the GM may impose disadvantage on this save.

**Comments:** This represents pushing a creature out of engagement with an ally, allowing them to escape Melee.

The Intelligence save represents the tactical nature of the action.

## Ranges

Characters in the Melee may make melee attacks against any other creature in the Melee.

Ranged attacks between zones count as the following range:
- Within the Close Combat zone: 20 ft
- Ranged to Close Combat, or vice versa: 50 ft
- Ranged zone to an opposing Ranged zone: 100 ft

Touch, or other short range spells, may be used between allies in the same zone (Close Combat or Ranged) that are not Engaged.

Otherwise the above ranges apply, e.g. to cast a spell on an ally from the Ranged zone to Close Combat is 50 ft of range, or to cast a touch spell on an ally who is Engaged, you must also enter the Melee.

Ranges beyond this, to the Out of Range zone, only apply in special circumstances, e.g. meteor swarm.

**Comments:** This means reach has little effect on zoned combat (unless it is 20 ft or more). The impact of differing weapon and spell ranges is also greatly reduced.

This simplification represents the chaotic nature of combat and that all movement is simultaneous. Rather than take a fixed snapshot in turn order when creatures may be a specific distance on a grid, the zoned system instead assumes some average distance based on zones.

The range cutoffs, 20 / 50 / 100, are intended so that a variety of spells and weapon ranges (both short and long) fall into the different categories.

You can always cast a spell or use a ranged weapon by moving closer (at greater risk of being Engaged).
